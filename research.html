Publications

Zamyatina et al. 2026: CH4 chemical pathways in Hot Jupiter atmospheres.

Analysis of CH4-CO interconversion pathways using graph theory, post-processed onto the output of Hot Jupiter general circulation models. In 3D, chemical quenching should be reframed as the transfer of control of the distribution of chemical species from thermochemistry to transport. In chemical disequilibrium the distribution of CH4 depends on (1) the CH4 abundance at the quench level, (2) where the flow trasnports CH4 and (3) whether local thermochemistry can remove that CH4.

Mukherjee et al. 2026: Limb Asymmetry observed in the atmosphere of WASP-94Ab.

Atmospheric characterisation of WASP-94Ab using JWST's NIRSpec instrument, revealing a morning limb covered in enstatite clouds and an evening limb with H2O absorption features. Explaining this observation from first principles requires a 3D model with coupled radiative transfer, fluid dynamics, gas-phase and aerosol-phase chemistry. Building such a model represents one of the the largest computational challenges in exoplanet science.

Christie et al. 2024: Dissipation Mechanisms in a 3D general circulation model applied to Hot Jupiters.

Atmospheric dynamics is sensative to the choice of dissipation and filtering required to maintian numerical stability within a GCM. Therefore observations are needed to constrain the magnitude of physical and numerical dissipation in Hot Jupiter atmospheres and inform modelling choices.

Zamyatina et al. 2024: Disequilibrium Chemistry and Metallicity in Hot Jupiter atmospheres.

Chemical quenching affects the spatial distribution of all chemical species, with CH4 zonally quenched and therefore particularly affected. Temperature changes, induced by increased atmospheric metallicity, push the quench level to lower pressures into the region dominated by the equatorial jet, causing equatorial depletion of species undergoing zonal quenching.
